Camp History

From 68 acres of woods, since 1944.

The story of Emmanuel Bible Camp, in the words of founding member Wilma Wahlstedt.

1943

Carl and Wilma Wahlstedt arrive in Kentucky to serve, taking over home-mission work in Barbourville.

May 1944

The Lord leads them to roughly 68 acres beside the highway — secluded yet central — purchased for $3,000.

Fall 1944

Home missionaries organize to begin a Bible camp together. On Oct. 16 the family moves onto the land — into an 8'×8' shed — and begins clearing trees.

June 1945

The first dining hall and dormitory go up. Camp opens with 48 campers — a most blessed first year.

In her own words

Written by Wilma Wahlstedt, a founding member of Emmanuel Bible Fellowship, on May 6, 1970.

Dear Friends at Home! You have, indeed, given quite a task when you asked our life's history. The Lord has certainly marvelously led — even though we have often failed Him, yet He has never failed us. Praise Him!

While we were in Connecticut, we met Rev. Otteson. He suggested we locate with them at least until we knew where the Lord would have us serve. So in January 1943 we arrived in Kentucky. We had no car, no furniture, only $50.00 promised monthly support. In April, Mr. Otteson got a letter from the Ruddy Larsons of Barbourville, saying they planned to leave the field for Oregon — would he know of someone to take their place? We decided to come and look over the field.

We were not satisfied to stay in rented houses, moving from one place to another as they were sold. So we began thinking how we might obtain land of our own. Then the Lord gave us the thought of a Bible camp. It seemed such a large undertaking. We looked all over the county, and then the Lord led us to the present location — secluded, yet right next to the highway, with natural gas, electricity, railroad, and telephone, centrally located. This we could have, about 68 acres, for $3,000.00. This was in May of 1944.

There were only woods, no buildings on the land, not even a road. We gathered the home missionaries together in the summer of 1944 and first organized to work together as a group and begin a Bible camp here. In the fall of 1944, the 16th of October, we moved onto the place, into a small 8'×8' shed. Carl built some lean-tos and we lived this way for four months until he had the Homestead built — a small cottage. It was quite a time of pioneering.

In April of 1945 the lumber came back from the mill. The dining room and kitchen were 18'×28', the dormitory 16'×50'. By camp time in June we had the buildings up. Two days before camp there was no water; the Lord supplied the pipe, and the men got water piped into a sink in time. We had only 48 campers that first year, but oh, it was a most blessed year, with all of them professing to accept the Lord.

During these 25 years we could cite many wonderful things the Lord has accomplished here, both materially and spiritually. We praise God for Emmanuel Bible Camp, where many have found the Lord and grown in Him. Many campers have gone on into the Lord's work. God bless you! Keep Praying!

In Christ,
Wilma Wahlstedt

Ministry Team

Taking the camp on the road.

A traveling team of EBC workers who share the love of God at churches and venues through music, drama, and more.

The EBC Ministry Team was born out of the camp workers' desire to spread the word about camp to the community.

In years past, the founding members visited schools, told Bible stories, and shared about camp. As those workers got older and could no longer visit, attendance declined — and many people assumed the camp had closed. A younger generation of workers decided to do something about it.

A small group of about ten began visiting churches in the spring before camp to sing a few songs and share information. The first year they traveled to about two churches; each year, more invited them to come. Skits were added, and more workers joined in. In the spring of 2004 the group officially became the Emmanuel Bible Camp Ministry Team, and has grown to 24 members.

The team uses a wide variety of ministry tools — interpretive movement, sign-art, skits, juggling, magic, music, and testimonies. Since 2004 they've ministered year-round, not only in churches but in schools, after-school programs, festivals, and youth events. Made up of both teenagers and adults, the team uses each member's gifts for God's glory.

FAQ

Everything parents ask.

Cost, schedule, what to pack, and how we handle homesickness — all in one place.

How long does camp last?
5 days and 4 nights. Registration begins Monday at 9:00am, and pickup is Friday evening at 4:00pm.
How much does it cost?
The fee for one week of camp is $75. Campers may also bring money for snacks from the camp store — most bring about $15.
How old does my child need to be?
Camp is grouped by grade across three weeks. See Our Program for the age range of each week.
What do I need to bring?

Bedding (single bed):

  • Pillow with pillowcase
  • Fitted sheet, top sheet, blanket
  • Sleeping bag (optional)

Toiletries:

  • Toothbrush, toothpaste, soap, shampoo/conditioner
  • Towel, washcloth, comb/brush

Clothing:

  • Knee-length shorts/capris or pants (6 pairs)
  • T-shirts or sleeveless shirts, at least 6 (high-neck tank tops only — no spaghetti straps or crop tops)
  • Underwear (at least 6) and socks (at least 6, more is better)
  • Tennis shoes (required daily for outdoor activities)
  • Flip-flops/sandals (optional), jacket or sweatshirt
  • Swimsuit (one-piece or full tankini for girls — no bikinis or revealing swimsuits)
  • One dress-up outfit for banquet night

Other:

  • Bible (New King James preferred — verses are learned from it)
  • Pen/pencil, notebook, refillable water bottle

What should I NOT bring?
  • Tobacco of any kind, lighters
  • Cell phones, iPhones, iPads, mp3 players, and other electronics
  • Radios, water guns
  • Short shorts, tank tops, spaghetti-strap shirts
  • Bikinis or swimsuits that show the midsection
How do you handle homesickness?
We encourage homesick campers to stay to the end of the week to truly experience camp, and our counselors are trained to help children through it. If a camper stays homesick, the Camp Director will contact you and keep you apprised, and we'll arrange pickup if you decide that's best. Parents are contacted before 8:00pm — and after 8:00pm only in a medical emergency.
What if I can't afford to send my child?
Need-based scholarships cover the cost of camp. We never turn a child away. A Scholarship Application is available here — or bring it with you to registration on Monday.
What is TNP?
Tuesday Night Praise — a time of worship and fellowship for teens and up, every Tuesday from 6–8pm during the school year.
What is the EBC Ministry Team?
A group, primarily of EBC workers, that travels to area churches and venues sharing the love of God through interpretive movement, drama, juggling, music, and more. See the Ministry Team page, or call (606) 546-4975.
How can I support Emmanuel Bible Camp?
You can donate time (summer staff, volunteers, year-round upkeep), donate resources (scholarships, groceries), or partner with us in prayer. See Serve @ EBC to get started.
